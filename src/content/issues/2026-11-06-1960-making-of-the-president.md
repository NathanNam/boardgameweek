Tuesday was Election Day. Across the country, voters chose all 435 members of the
House, about a third of the Senate and dozens of governors. Democrats won control of
both chambers of Congress, and a few races, including a Senate seat in Mississippi,
were still too close to call days later. Whatever your family thinks of the results,
it's a good week to talk about how an election actually works: where candidates
spend their time, what wins votes, and why every count matters.

In *1960: The Making of the President* one player is John F. Kennedy and the other is
Richard Nixon, in one of the closest presidential races in American history. You move
your candidate around the map, campaign state by state, prepare for the first
televised debates and spend your cards on either real events from 1960 or more
campaigning. It's a two-player game with real decisions, and every card is a small
history lesson.
